# Connection-methods research record

## Project and objective

- **Project:** Robot Royale meltybrain.
- **Objective:** Research compact, lightweight ways to retain parts and maintain electrical connections under sustained rotation and combat impacts.
- **Requirement:** Wiring, connections and internal mounts must remain secure; the sensor's measurement geometry also matters.
- **Status:** Source research and illustrative calculations completed. Design selection, representative joints and hardware validation remain unfinished.

## Requirements and constraints

- Neutral method descriptions and tradeoffs; no ranking, selected class, final design, product or supplier recommendations.
- Mechanical joints, printed features, bonding, internal mounting, electrical terminations, connector retention, wire routing, board support, power connections and design principles.
- Six fields for each method/application, a comparison table, reported failure/repair evidence and inspectable sources.
- Distinguish engineering sources, builder reports, modeled results, planned changes, calculations and missing evidence.

## Research stage — 8 November 2026

- **Attempted:** Trace original combat/melty build logs and failure analyses, then connect them to primary engineering guidance.
- **Changed:** Expanded the folder's initial scope record into a full report. Separated electrical contact, terminal retention, connector mating retention, harness anchoring, component-body support and chassis load paths.
- **Result:** 35 six-field method/application records: 15 mechanical, 12 electrical and 8 internal-mounting applications. The report has 59 distinct linked sources, 13 named-builder mechanical incident records and four separately qualified historical project-page records. Electrical failures and remedies are retained within the relevant methods and interface map.
- **Calculations (reasoning):** A 5 g component at 30 mm and 4,000 RPM requires about 26.3 N of steady radial restraint, approximately 537 g acceleration. An idealized outward sensor-radius shift illustrates possible rate/heading error. Neither example bounds combat impacts or selects an operating point.
- **Evidence limits:** Several methods lack a transferable strength measurement or a precise named combat example. Failed repairs, limited fight outcomes, intended improvements and model results remain distinct.
- **Remaining issues:** Actual geometry, mass distribution, RPM, materials/process, temperatures, service cycles, event requirements, load cases and acceptance criteria are not specified.

## Review and validation

- **Confirmed by source/document inspection:** Original builder reports and primary engineering references were located; access limits are recorded. Two focused reviews found no further actionable mechanical/electrical issues.
- **Document checks:** Method IDs, six-field structure, comparison-table coverage, internal navigation and linked local assets checked. Arithmetic checked independently against the saved inputs.
- **Unresolved:** Pullout, fatigue, creep, vibration, impact, thermal behavior, electrical intermittency, sensor displacement, retention and full-assembly survival.
- **Not performed:** Physical testing, firmware validation, CAD/FEA of a proposed robot, joint fabrication or combat qualification.
