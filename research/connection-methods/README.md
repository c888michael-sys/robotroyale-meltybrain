# Connection methods research

Research how electrical and mechanical connections can remain secure during full-body rotation, vibration and combat impacts.

## Requirement recorded — 8 November 2026

**User requirement:** Structural integrity and secure wiring are essential because the robot experiences high g-forces. Wires, connectors and mounted components must not become loose during operation.

**Status:** Research area created. Connection methods have not yet been selected or validated.

## Research scope

- Wire terminations: soldered joints, crimped contacts and other connection methods; their documented failure modes.
- Strain relief and cable routing: supporting wires, protecting board pads, avoiding abrasion, and retaining slack.
- Connector retention: locking features, secondary restraints, contact security and service access.
- Component mounting: controller boards, sensors, receivers, motor controllers, LEDs, batteries and heavier electrical components.
- Mechanical joints: fasteners, nuts, inserts, captive features, shaft/wheel retention and joints in plastic structures.
- Bonding and encapsulation: adhesives, joint-locking materials, potting and their effects on repair, cooling and sensor placement.
- Load paths and geometry: continuous rotational loading, impact loading, mounting radius, balance and movement between connected parts.
- Inspection and validation: checking for movement, intermittent contacts, damaged insulation and loosening before and after testing.

These are topics to investigate, not approved construction methods. Record sources, reported failures, engineering reasoning and actual test results separately. Identify which methods are electrical connections, mechanical retention, or both.
